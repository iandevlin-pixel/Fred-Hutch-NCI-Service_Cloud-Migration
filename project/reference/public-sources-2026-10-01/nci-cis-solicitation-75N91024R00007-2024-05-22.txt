ARTICLE B.1. BRIEF DESCRIPTION OF SUPPLIES OR SERVICES

The Contractor shall operate the National Cancer Institute’s (NCI) Contact Center, also known as NCI’s Cancer
Information Service (CIS), by responding to inquiries on behalf of NCI, an essential part of NCI’s cancer education
and information efforts. The Contractor shall provide scientifically based, unbiased information to patients, their
families and friends, physicians, and other health professionals and the public about all aspects of cancer. The
type of information includes cancer research and clinical trials, cancer prevention, risk factors, symptoms, early
detection, diagnosis, treatment, living with cancer, survivorship, and tobacco and cessation counseling. The
Contractor shall also provide information about NCI programs/grants to researchers/investigators and specialists
to address first tier questions of the research community. The Contractor shall provide and manage a bilingual
(English and Spanish) omni-channel Contact Center.

Hard Metric - The replacement of a standard inch-pound size with an accepted metric size for a

produced, and described in inch-pound values with soft metric values also shown for information or
comparison purposes.

                           SECTION M – EVALUATION METHOD AND FACTORS
GENERAL

   Selection of an Offeror for the contract award will be based on evaluation of proposals against five factors.
   The factors in order of importance are:

       1.   Mandatory qualification
       2.   Technical
       3.   Past performance
       4.   Section 508 compliance
       5.   Cost

   The mandatory qualification must be met by the time of receipt of the Final Proposal Revision for an Offeror
   to be considered for award. Although technical factors are of paramount consideration in the award of the
   contract, past performance and cost are also important to the overall contract award decision. All evaluation
   factors other than cost or price, when combined are significantly more important than cost. The
   Government intends to make an award to that Offeror whose proposal provides the best overall value to the
   Government.

   The evaluation will be based on the demonstrated capabilities of the prospective Contractors in relation to
   the needs of the project as set forth in the Solicitation. The merits of each proposal will be evaluated
   carefully. Each proposal must document the feasibility of successful implementation of the requirements of
   the Solicitation. Offerors must submit information sufficiently to evaluate their proposals based on the
   detailed factors listed below.

COST/PRICE EVALUATION

   Offeror’s cost/price proposal will be evaluated for reasonableness; technically unacceptable Offerors will not
   be reviewed. For a price to be reasonable, it must represent a price to the Government that a prudent
   person would pay when consideration is given to prices in the market. Normally, price reasonableness is
   established through adequate price competition, but may also be determined through cost and price
   analysis techniques as described in FAR 15.404.

   Cost Realism: The specific elements of each Offeror(s) proposed costs are realistic when the proposed cost
   elements are evaluated and found to 1) be realistic for the work to be performed; 2) reflect a clear
   understanding of the requirements; and 3) be consistent with the unique methods of performance and
   materials described in the Offeror(s) technical proposal.
   Cost Realism will be evaluated only on the Offeror(s) inputs which the Government will use to determine the
   most probable cost to perform the contract in a manner consistent with the Offeror’s proposal. Cost realism
   analysis will be conducted in accordance with FAR 15.404-1(d). The result of the cost realism analysis will be
   considered in the making the best value tradeoff decision.
