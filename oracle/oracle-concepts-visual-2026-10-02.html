How Oracle works, for a Salesforce team

Eight pictures of the Oracle ideas that trip up a Salesforce reader, each next to its Salesforce counterpart. The full term-by-term reference is in the companion document.

Oracle Service Cloud, what CIS has todaySalesforce, what we're buildingApplies to both

1. The profile does far more

One Oracle profile sets access, screens, queues and how work is handed out.

2. A queue is a field value

An inquiry has a queue and an assigned person at once. It isn't the owner.

3. No record types

One screen layout shows and hides tabs by rule.

4. An interface is a copy of the setup

English and Spanish are two configurations over one database.

5. Logic lives in two places

Rules on the screen, then rules on the server. The server wins.

2A queue in Oracle, a queue in Salesforce

Same word, different thing. In Oracle the queue stays on the record after someone takes it. In Salesforce the queue is the owner until someone takes it, and then it's gone.

Oracle

QueueEnglish Cancer Phone Call
Assigned toA specialist

StatusCompleted

Queue and assignee sit side by side, and both stay. The queue is a value from a menu.

Who sets the queueA business rule (email, chat), or the specialist by hand (phone)
Who can work itThe profile lists the queues, ranks them, and sets how many to take
How work arrivesSpecialists pull it: they click Fill Inbox. Chats are pushed, from a separate list of chat queues

StatusNew
↓a specialist accepts it
Case 00001234, being worked
OwnerA specialist

The owner is a queue or a person, never both. The queue name is lost on accept unless a field keeps it.

QueueHolds waiting records as their owner
Omni-Channel routingPushes work to an available specialist, by queue or by skill
Record type or fieldSays what kind of inquiry it is, for good
At CIS today, the phone queue is a label. Cisco routes the call. The specialist then opens an inquiry and picks the service number and queue by hand, so the queue records what kind of call it was. In the new system Amazon Connect holds the phone queues, and "what kind of call" becomes a record type and fields on the Case.

3One Oracle profile becomes six Salesforce things

CIS has 40 profiles because each one bundles language, program, chat and knowledge rights. In Salesforce those are separate settings that combine.

Oracle profileExample: Bilingual Calls + Dual Chat + Knowledge
→
ProfileBase access and login rules
Permission setsOne per capability: Spanish, clinical trials chat, knowledge editing
Lightning appThe navigation and utility bar. Replaces the navigation set
Page assignmentWhich record page each app and record type gets. Replaces the workspace
Queue membership and skillsSet on the queue or the person, not the profile
Presence configurationCapacity and statuses for Omni-Channel. Replaces pull policy and inbox limit
What it means: the 40 profiles shouldn't be copied. A handful of profiles plus permission sets for language, program and knowledge rights covers the same ground.

5Where the rules run

Whether a field is visible or required is decided in layers. A later layer can undo an earlier one, so a field's own settings don't tell you how it behaves.

Oracle: four layers

1The fieldWho can see and edit it, and whether it's required
2The workspaceHidden, required or read-only on this screen
3Workspace rulesRun on the screen as the specialist works. Show, hide, require, set a value
↓the specialist saves
4Business rulesRun on the server. Set the queue, status and fields, send replies. They win over everything before
Custom code (PHP)Runs when a record changes, or on a schedule

Salesforce: the nearest equivalents

1Field-level securityWho can see and edit the field
2Page layout and Dynamic FormsWhat's on the page, required or read-only
3Dynamic Forms visibilityShows and hides. It can't set a value while the specialist types
↓the specialist saves
4Validation rules and FlowsBefore-save Flow sets values. After-save Flow, assignment rules and Omni-Channel flows route and notify
Apex, scheduled FlowOnly where Flow can't do it
Watch for: Oracle workspace rules that fill in a value live, such as converting the client's time to Seattle time. In Salesforce that happens on save, or needs a small component.

7Sibling articles, or translations

Oracle pairs an English and a Spanish article as siblings: two separate articles that point at each other. Salesforce treats Spanish as a language version of one article, which needs a master.

Oracle: two records, linked

Answer 4413English. Its own text, status and owner
↔
Answer 4848Spanish. Its own text, status and owner

Siblings share products, categories and attachments. Either one can exist alone.

Salesforce: one article, two languages

Knowledge articleOne article number
↓
English versionThe master
Spanish versionA translation of the master

A translation can't exist without its master article.

The catch: of 1,594 live Spanish articles, 770 have an English sibling and 824 don't. Those 824 have no master to hang from, so either they become master articles in Spanish or CIS pairs them first.

8Retention: scrubbed, not deleted

CIS describes contacts as deleted at 15 months. The data shows the contact stays and its personal details are removed.

Day 0Contact created with the inquiry: name, phone, email, address
Months 0 to 15Details visible. A returning caller's history can be found
Month 15A script replaces the name, empties the address, flags the contact, and hides the inquiry's messages
AfterThe contact row and the inquiry stay for good, with coding intact for reporting

Oracle

A custom PHP scriptStored in File Manager, run on a schedule. Kicksaw hasn't read it yet

Salesforce

A scheduled Flow or Apex jobBlanks the same fields, sets a flag, and hides or deletes the messages. Salesforce has no built-in rule for this
Evidence: every contact created through March 2025 is flagged as scrubbed and every one from May 2025 on isn't, in a copy taken in July 2026. One thing to ask CIS: about half of scrubbed contacts still have a value in the email field.
