CIS Callback Lifecycle

How a callback gets created, worked and closed in Oracle today, pieced together from the client's own walkthroughs. It covers callbacks only. Follow-up surveys are a separate feature, fenced off below.

Drawn from the client's discovery walkthroughs.

Two words that get mixed up

Callback

A scheduled check-in call, at an exact date and time the client agreed to. Callbacks are numbered 1, 2, 3 and so on through the series.

Attempt

One try at reaching the client for a given callback. Each callback gets up to three. "A callback attempt doesn't mean you called them... We tried."

In Oracle, each attempt is its own task. The unit of work is the attempt, and the callback number is a label carried on each task.

Rules that ride on the task

  • Time zoneThe specialist records the client's local time zone and time. Workspace rules convert it to Seattle time and handle daylight saving. Clients span the US and its territories, including Guam.
  • Fields by lineThe task workspace shows or hides fields depending on whether the task is VA or clinical trials.
  • Own fields, not inquiry codingCallbacks are the one interaction type that skips the universal inquiry coding. They carry their own fields on the task.
  • Context lives on the inquiryTo work a callback, the specialist opens the task and clicks through to the original inquiry.
  • Manual dialingNothing dials a callback automatically and nothing puts one in a queue. Specialists dial by hand from the report.
  • Entry pointsCallback 1 comes from the intake call or from a VA Direct referral, which specialists paste into Oracle by hand before creating the callback task.
  • Text remindersThe callback report goes to Amazon Pinpoint, which texts the client 24 hours and 1 hour before. The texts carry no PII or PHI, replies get an automated "call us" response, and delivery status writes back to Oracle. Texts go only to clients who gave SMS consent and are enrolled in callbacks.
  • ShiftsSpecialists on a callback shift take no inbound calls, and otherwise have the same skills as everyone else. Clinical trials specialists have their own callback shifts and also work the CCR web-form email queue during them.

Looks like a callback, is not

Separate feature

Follow-up surveys

Tobacco only. Calls at roughly 4, 7 and 13 months to check whether the client quit. Built as Oracle surveys embedded in the workspace, not tasks, and worked from their own report. Consent comes from a question on the demographics survey. Nobody knows why the VA requires phone rather than an emailed link.

Shares the shift

Proactive calls

Outreach that Fred Hutch starts, scheduled as tasks and worked on the same callback shifts. No series agreed with the client, and the attempt rules are unknown. May be the "ProActor" queue seen on the Oracle tour.

Shares the shift

CCR web-form follow-up

When a clinical trials web form is missing information, the specialist on the clinical trials callback shift calls or emails the client. It is a single try, possibly followed by an email, with no series.

Design questions to work through

Framing only, no answers. These are the choices the Salesforce design turns on.

  • Record shape. One record per attempt, as Oracle does it, or a callback record with its attempts beneath it? Avi's framing: who needs to be contacted, then how many tries and the outcome of each.
  • Who creates the next task. A specialist by hand, as today, or automation driven by the attempt outcome: 15 minutes, next business day, end of series?
  • How work reaches people. A report worked from the top, or routed work that goes to whoever is on a callback shift?
  • Where time zones live. Where the local-to-Seattle conversion happens, and what reference time the shifts use once they are defined outside Oracle.
  • End states. What a completed series and an ended series leave behind, and how a client who calls in later starts a new one.
  • Extending it. What it would take to switch callbacks on for general cancer without rebuilding.
